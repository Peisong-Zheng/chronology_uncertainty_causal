## Figure 00 — Model, selected records and realized chronology mismatch

**Files:** `figures/04_check_tbk_chronology/00_model_and_records.png` and `.pdf`

**Caption.** Four-box TBK model and the chronology perturbations applied to its southern and northern endpoint records. (a) Neighbouring boxes exchange temperature through diffusion, with additional global-mean restoring that introduces reciprocal dependence among all boxes; the schematic arrows show neighbour diffusion rather than the complete equation graph. Transport is externally prescribed and acts directly on T2–T4; independent process noise enters each physical box update, and the later pairwise analyses observe only South (T1, X) and North (T4, Y). (b) All four accurately dated temperature trajectories are shown for a fixed 15–12 kyr BP illustration window in the first simulated history; temperatures retain their original model units and the synthetic age origin does not identify particular historical DO events. (c) Solid curves show the first accepted southern and northern age members at per-layer proposal SD 200 years, and dotted curves show their respective uncertain observed centres. (d) Dots show the actual inter-record temporal RMS age mismatch over 29–1 kyr for all three histories and ten members per nonzero level; horizontal displacement separates points and has no scientific meaning. Dashed guides show the idealized pointwise SD of the difference between two independently perturbed, two-layer age members, 2σ, which is distinct from each realized temporal RMS and is not an uncertainty bound. No proxy values, model dynamics or forcing times change between chronology levels.

## Figure 01 — Directional evidence as chronology uncertainty increases

**Files:** `figures/04_check_tbk_chronology/01_evidence_vs_uncertainty.png` and `.pdf`

**Caption.** Changes in the complete two-direction evidence state with increasing uncertainty in both endpoint chronologies. Panels (a–d) show PCMCI+, LKIF, Granger causality and lag-screened CCM; stacked bars give mutually exclusive states as percentages of all requested inputs, with segment counts printed where space permits. State classification first retains failed/unavailable inputs, then unresolved evidence, followed by both retained directions, South-to-North only, North-to-South only, or neither retained direction. Panel (e) compares the fraction retaining both directions without unresolved evidence, using the same requested denominators: three accurate-age histories at zero uncertainty and thirty inputs, comprising three histories and ten members each, at every nonzero level. The categorical positions are equally spaced for legibility, while labels give the numerical proposal SD; joining lines guide comparison rather than implying a continuous calibrated response. Unresolved evidence can coexist with a retained directional arrow: the PCMCI+ annotation pools all 123 requested inputs and reports unresolved contemporaneous links in 123/123, alongside retained lagged South-to-North and North-to-South arrows in 122/123 each. These yellow states therefore do not imply absence of lagged directional evidence, and the member fractions do not constitute thirty independent histories. Bidirectional evidence retention describes the specified statistical pipelines; it is not full-network recovery, a feedback-detection probability, or a claim that a one-way result proves physical one-way coupling.

**Companion evidence.** `baseline_transitions.csv` retains every history's accurate state and its subsequent member states. A both-to-one numerator must use only histories whose accurate state was both, with its explicit member denominator; baseline one-way histories remain in the all-history figures and must not be described as chronology-induced loss of bidirectionality. `scenario_summary.csv` preserves marginal directional fractions and within-history usable counts/support.

## Figure 02 — Paired changes in native scores

**Files:** `figures/04_check_tbk_chronology/02_paired_score_changes.png` and `.pdf`

**Caption.** Native method scores under uncertain dates relative to the accurate-age score of the same simulated history. Columns show PCMCI+, LKIF, Granger causality and CCM, with South-to-North in the upper row and North-to-South in the lower row. Each dot is one finite, successful member-minus-reference score difference, including nonsignificant results; horizontal displacement separates points, black horizontal marks give the median, and black vertical segments span the interquartile range across the displayed members. Dashed zero lines indicate an unchanged native score, and each tick gives the per-layer proposal SD and usable/requested pair count. The three-history, ten-member distributions are descriptive and are not confidence intervals; the methods retain their own score definitions and panel scales. PCMCI+ uses the maximum GPDC over all cross-lag entries from 0 to 5 steps, including nonsignificant entries; LKIF uses the maximum absolute normalized information flow over its five native lag indices; Granger causality uses the logarithm of the restricted-to-full residual sum-of-squares ratio. CCM uses its original Tp=0 mean reconstruction skill ρ, not the maximum across the lag scan or a physical coupling strength.

## Figure 03 — CCM significance, lag screening and timing evidence

**Files:** `figures/04_check_tbk_chronology/03_ccm_evidence_and_timing.png` and `.pdf`

**Caption.** Distinct components of CCM evidence for South-to-North (left column) and North-to-South (right column). (a,b) Curves separate original Tp=0 significance, retention after the lag screen, and raw-significant cases whose lag evidence is unresolved or incompatible; all use the requested directional-input denominator, with three accurate references and thirty inputs at each nonzero level. (c,d) For each level, curves give the median of saved case lag profiles: each case profile first averages reconstruction skill across one hundred paired, fixed-length L=300 libraries, then the displayed median is taken across cases, without re-fitting or selecting significant results. Target shifts span −600 to +600 years in 20-year steps; the legend counts available case profiles, and a vertical dotted line marks zero shift. (e,f) Stacked bars average the saved fractions of library peaks classified as past, future, boundary, zero, tied or failed across all requested inputs, including those without original significance; missing library evidence contributes to the failed/unavailable category rather than being discarded. The negative interior-peak rule and 90% library-sign threshold act on each individual input, not the median profile or the averaged category fractions. A rejected, unresolved or nonsignificant direction does not establish absence of physical feedback, and significance against the existing cut-and-swap surrogate construction does not remove every possible shared-periodic-forcing explanation.
