All five production PNGs and all five independently rendered one-page PDFs were inspected. ## Shared design and reading conventions

All twelve inputs have accurate ages. Conditions A, B and C use annual independent process-noise increment SDs of 0.005, 0.05 and 0.5, respectively. Condition D subtracts the known, same-parameter noise-free trajectory from the baseline trajectory. D is a model-assisted diagnostic, not a preprocessing method proposed for real proxy records. The temperature transition operator, externally prescribed transport, integration interval, sampling, analysis window and estimator settings are unchanged. Stronger noise is an identification experiment rather than a physically calibrated climate scenario.

R1, R2 and R3 denote simulation seeds 20261009, 20261301 and 20261302. Noise draws are paired across conditions within each history. South is T1/X and North is T4/Y. Three histories are shown individually wherever comparisons use three results; there are no chronology members, and no displayed spread is an inferential confidence interval. All causal inputs contain 1401 observations at 20-year intervals over 29–1 kyr BP. Older ages are plotted on the left.

## Figure 00 — What changes when stochastic excitation increases?

**Files:** `figures/04_1_check_tbk_excitation/00_excitation_and_inputs.png` and `.pdf`.

**Caption.** Accurately dated model trajectories under three paired noise amplitudes and known-forcing subtraction. (a,b) Southern and northern annual trajectories for the preselected first history in a fixed 15–12 kyr BP window. The dashed reference is the same model integrated with zero process noise; coloured trajectories use conditions A–C. (c) The baseline-minus-noise-free response, condition D, is shown on its own temperature-difference scale so that small stochastic fluctuations remain visible. (d) For each endpoint and each of the three histories, the standard deviation of the stochastic response divided by that of the noise-free forced response, using the actual 1401 sampled times over 29–1 kyr BP. This is a descriptive amplitude ratio, not a fitted causal strength or a formal decomposition of observed variance: the realized random and forced responses need not be uncorrelated. The vertical axis is logarithmic; the horizontal line marks equal component standard deviations. Condition D is omitted from this ratio panel because its analysis input explicitly excludes the known forced response. Pairing and unchanged dynamics make this an excitation diagnostic while preserving the model's physical memory.

## Figure 01 — Directional retention and unresolved evidence are separate

**Files:** `figures/04_1_check_tbk_excitation/01_directional_evidence.png` and `.pdf`.

**Caption.** Native directional decisions for each of twelve accurately dated endpoint inputs. Panels (a–d) show PCMCI+/GPDC, LKIF, Granger causality and lag-screened CCM. Rows distinguish the four conditions and all three histories, with dividing lines separating conditions. The first two columns show retention of South-to-North and North-to-South, respectively. The third column separately records unresolved evidence and therefore does not overwrite a retained arrow. For PCMCI+, this column indicates an unoriented or conflicting contemporaneous native graph link; the first two columns can simultaneously retain lagged arrows. For CCM, it indicates raw-significant evidence whose lag screen is unresolved in at least one direction. GC and LKIF have no analogous unresolved category in their present wrappers, so this column is marked not assessed. Missing or failed evidence is displayed separately. These are pipeline-specific evidence states, not comparable estimates of graph-recovery accuracy; retaining both directions is not sufficient to establish the complete physical feedback network.

## Figure 02 — PCMCI+ evidence at each tested lag

**Files:** `figures/04_1_check_tbk_excitation/02_pcmci_lag_evidence.png` and `.pdf`.

**Caption.** Lag-resolved PCMCI+ output rather than a single maximum score. Left and right columns show South-to-North and North-to-South, respectively. Panels (a,b) show GPDC for every condition and history; panels (c,d) show the separately authorized native ParCorr comparison for the three original baseline inputs only. Columns span lag zero through five, corresponding to 0–100 years at the fixed analysis step. Cell colour is the saved native conditional-dependence statistic, including nonsignificant entries; GPDC and ParCorr have separate colour scales and their magnitudes are not interchangeable. Cell symbols show the graph after the existing directional Holm correction: arrows retain native orientation, o–o denotes an unresolved contemporaneous link, x–x a conflict, and a dot no retained graph link. At zero lag the two directional panels describe the same contemporaneous relation. Dense rows of lagged arrows indicate broad lag retention within the tested window, not identification of five separate physical mechanisms. ParCorr is a baseline conditional-test diagnostic and does not replace GPDC in the experiment.

## Figure 03 — High CCM reconstruction skill need not resolve timing

**Files:** `figures/04_1_check_tbk_excitation/03_ccm_reconstruction_and_timing.png` and `.pdf`.

**Caption.** CCM reconstruction and temporal compatibility for South-to-North (left) and North-to-South (right). (a,b) Thin curves show each history's saved mean reconstruction skill across 100 paired L=300 lag libraries; thick curves give a descriptive median of the three case profiles within each condition. The 61 target shifts run from −600 to +600 years in 20-year increments, with zero marked by a vertical guide. Legend counts distinguish original Tp=0 significance against 100 cut-and-swap surrogates from retention after the lag screen; each denominator is the three requested histories. Native CCM strength remains original Tp=0 skill, not the highest point on these curves. (c,d) Each stacked bar is one input's 100 requested libraries, classified by peak location as negative interior, positive interior, scan boundary, zero, tied or failed. Missing library evidence contributes to failed/unavailable rather than disappearing from the denominator. Bars include inputs without original significance. Negative interior peaks support temporal compatibility only under the existing per-input 90% sign rule; this rule is not applied to a pooled bar or median profile and does not upgrade an originally nonsignificant result. High reconstruction skill can reflect shared forced trajectories; these displays distinguish reconstruction, surrogate significance and lag compatibility without treating any of them as a calibrated physical-feedback probability.

## Figure 04 — Does the fitted GC model leave structured residuals?

**Files:** `figures/04_1_check_tbk_excitation/04_gc_residual_diagnostics.png` and `.pdf`.

**Caption.** Diagnostics of the fixed native VAR(1) fits used for Granger causality, with no additional regression fitting. (a,b) Residual autocorrelation for the southern and northern equations over 20–500 years. Thin curves retain all three histories and thick curves are condition-wise descriptive medians; the zero guide denotes no residual correlation at the displayed lag. (c) Contemporaneous correlation between the two equations' residuals, with one point per history and horizontal displacement used only for visibility. (d) Negative base-ten logarithm of the saved native multivariate residual-whiteness p value, with a dashed guide at p=0.05. Higher values indicate stronger evidence against the fitted residuals being white; an exactly zero software p value is displayed at 300 and does not mean a mathematically exact zero probability. Residual serial dependence indicates dynamics or forcing left unexplained by this fixed-order observational model. Contemporaneous residual correlation is a separate diagnostic and does not alone establish a causal direction. These checks qualify the directional GC results in Figure 01; they neither delete an inconvenient result nor demonstrate that a selected alternative model is valid.

## Verified reading checks

- Figure 01 retains the baseline GPDC lagged arrows alongside unresolved contemporaneous links; yellow cells do not hide directional retention. It also preserves baseline LKIF South-to-North non-retention and the two retained North-to-South CCM results.
- Figure 02 keeps every tested lag, the baseline ParCorr reverse-oriented lag-zero links, and its one conflicting contemporaneous result. The zero-link condition C and sparse condition D are visible rather than filtered out.
- Figure 03 preserves all 24 requested directional profiles and all 2400 requested library-peak classifications. Legends sit outside the curves. The lower reconstruction skill and unstable timing of D remain visible even where its original surrogate test is significant.
- Figure 04 shows all twelve residual diagnostics. Condition C and D each still reject native residual whiteness in two of three histories at p<0.05; weak residual contemporaneous correlation is not presented as proof of a sufficient GC model.
- Panel labels, complete legends, noise-amplitude labels, history counts, older-left age axes and English text were checked in both export formats. The minor post-run corrections affect labels, spacing and legend placement only. No original 04 figures or numerical native results were changed.
