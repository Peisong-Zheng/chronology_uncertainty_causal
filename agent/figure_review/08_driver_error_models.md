## Figure 00 — Prescribed and realized Z-age uncertainty

**Files:** `00_error_models.png` and `00_error_models.pdf`

**Caption.** Prescribed Z-age uncertainty and the resulting chronology diagnostics for the four error types. (a) Proposal standard-deviation envelopes are defined over the retained 0-30 kyr BP record. C is constant; L and E increase toward older ages, reaching 40 years at 30 kyr BP; E uses the existing curvature parameter of two. J has five fixed smooth local peaks with a prescribed maximum of 10 years and intervals of 2.8 kyr each. Their total interval width is 50% of the 29-1 kyr analysis window; this is not a threshold-exceedance coverage statistic, and discrete sample ages need not intersect the precise peak centres. Grey strips outside 29-1 kyr identify retained endpoint regions excluded from the causal analysis. (b) For each type, colours distinguish the three complete age schemes. Filled circles give the median RMS error relative to true ages among the ten accepted members, vertical lines span their minimum and maximum, and open diamonds give the RMS error of the uncertain observed centre. Dashed segments give the RMS of the proposal-SD envelope on the same analysis grid. These proposal and realized quantities are distinct; the proposal SD is not a bound on realized errors. (c) Bars show the analysis-window mean of pointwise population standard deviations across the ten accepted chronology members, and labels give the number accepted divided by the number of whole curves attempted. This member spread describes the ensemble around its own mean, not accuracy relative to truth. The three schemes are reused across the three simulated histories and both generating systems. Members are drawn around uncertain observed ages and accepted only when stratigraphic order is preserved. All diagnostics in (b,c) use 29-1 kyr BP. Types retain their original unequal overall scales, so comparisons concern the complete prescribed error settings rather than an isolated envelope-shape effect; min-max member ranges are descriptive, not confidence intervals.

## Reading notes

- Start with Figure 00 before comparing causal performance: C/L/E/J are not RMS-matched, and J has a lower prescribed peak SD.
- Read across a realization row in Figure 01 to compare the complete error settings while keeping X/Y, dynamics and the estimator seed fixed. Compare with that row's Acc result before attributing a problem to Z age uncertainty.
- Use Figure 02 to check whether an apparent type effect is shared by all three schemes or concentrated in a particular scheme/history combination.
- U distinguishes unresolved evidence from a definite wrong-arrow state. Correct and U are mutually exclusive, but unresolved evidence can coexist with a retained arrow in the detailed scientific results.
- A3, for example, identifies the same random-generation setting across types; it does not mean that C, L, E and J have the same realized age errors or that their accepted members are necessarily proposal-by-proposal matches.
